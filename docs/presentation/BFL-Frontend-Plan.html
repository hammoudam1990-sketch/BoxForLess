1 · Summary

One platform for ordering, balances, expiry and reports, with Odoo as the master

Customers and sales reps order from a phone. Staff approve, follow up and report from a desktop. Every figure comes from Odoo; the platform adds the screens and the rules Odoo does not have.

3,883active products in the catalogue today (8,933 in Odoo in total)
504customers already loaded, each with an access code
18screens: 9 for customers, 2 for sales reps, 7 for staff

What already works today (version 0.4.2)

  • A customer catalogue on the phone: search, categories, stock status, carton quantities and a request cart.
  • Access codes per customer, a staff admin with a dashboard, imports from the Odoo Excel export, and 290 automated tests.

What the new front end adds

  • Proper customer accounts instead of access codes, for credit customers (companies) and cash customers.
  • Prices per carton from each customer's Odoo price class, shown only to approved customers.
  • Orders that land in Odoo as quotations for the Sales Manager to approve, with the same order number in both systems.
  • The customer's balance, invoices and a credit-limit check before an order is sent.
  • Expiry control in the warehouse and on customers' shelves, with discounts approved by the Sales Manager.
  • Payment reminders on each customer's own schedule, and monthly reports for the CEO and managers.
What we ask for at this meeting
  1. Agreement on the screens and journeys in this document.
  2. A go-ahead for Phase 0: the budget, the two developers, the hosting and Odoo API access.
  3. The sales team's help with the Odoo data clean-up (section 10) and the open policies.
2 · How it fits with Odoo

Odoo stays the master; the platform reads it and sends back only two things

The platform keeps a local copy of what it reads from Odoo, so screens are fast and keep working if Odoo is slow. It never edits Odoo's data. The only writes are draft quotations and, after approval, new credit customers. A person approves every order in Odoo.

The platform never
  • confirms, sends or cancels an order
  • writes invoices, payments or stock moves
  • changes prices, credit limits or products
  • reads cost prices
Safety built in
  • built and tested on a copy of Odoo first
  • one integration user; disabling it stops everything
  • orders queue if Odoo is down, and never duplicate
  • a daily report compares BFL with Odoo

4 · Journey 1

A credit customer orders

From registration to a paid invoice. The customer always sees the Odoo order number, and the order changes their balance only when Odoo posts the invoice.

5 · Journey 2

A cash customer orders

In Odoo, cash buyers share one account, and each buyer's name, phone and address go on their order. The platform keeps every cash buyer's own details, so they still get a normal account on BFL.

Privacy rule for the shared cash account

Because many buyers share it, BFL never shows that account's balance, invoices or order history to anyone. A cash customer sees only the orders they placed on BFL. Cash sales entered directly in Odoo cannot be linked to a buyer, so they are not shown.

6 · Journey 3

Expiry: in our warehouse and on the customer's shelf

In the warehouse, every lot gets an expiry band (warning periods per category; shorter for 3 to 6 month products). Each lot in a band gets an action and an owner on the expiry list (S03). Short-dated stock moves to a clearance location and is sold on BFL at a discount, marked "not returnable for expiry".

On the customer's shelf, every delivered lot is tracked from the moment the truck is loaded. Before it expires, the rep visits the shop:

Customers can also set a minimum shelf life (for example 90 days). If only shorter-dated stock is available, they see the expiry date and must tick to accept it before ordering.

7 · Journey 4

Payment reminders on each customer's schedule

The platform reminds; it does not collect. Customers pay as they do today and Finance records payments in Odoo. The Sales Manager sets each credit customer's schedule on S07.

Reminders go by email first; SMS or WhatsApp once a provider is chosen. Each one lists only that customer's own open invoices, and every reminder is logged.

Credit check before an order is sent: available credit = credit limit − amount owed − open orders not yet invoiced. Example: limit 10,000, owed 6,000, an earlier order of 2,500 waiting for approval leaves GHS 1,500, not 4,000.

9 · How the front end is built

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, phone first, built on what already works

The customer catalogue was rewritten in October 2026 without any framework, and every new screen follows the same approach: no build step and nothing loaded from outside, so pages are light on low-end phones and slow networks, and there is less to break or update.

Building blocks shared by all screens

ComponentUsed on
Header and phone menuEvery customer and rep screen
Tab bar with request badgeC03 to C09
Product card and carton stepper (− number +)C03, C04, C05, C08; already built and tested
Status pill (waiting, approved, dispatched, invoiced, paid, overdue)C06, C07, C09, staff lists
Staff sidebar, tables, filters and CSV downloadS01 to S07
Form fields with labels and error messagesC01, C02, C05, R02, S01, S02, S07

Rules every screen follows

  • Security on the server: who is signed in, which customer they belong to and what they may see is decided by the server on every request. Hiding on screen is never enough.
  • No data leaks: automated tests check that no customer ever receives another customer's data, cost prices, hidden products or company figures.
  • Typing never breaks: a field someone types into is built once and never redrawn, so the phone keyboard never closes mid-typing.
  • Accessible: real buttons and labelled fields, readable contrast, touch targets of at least 44 px.
  • Slow networks: loading placeholders instead of blank pages; an order is saved at once and sent to Odoo in the background.
  • The current look: white, ink black and burnt orange, as in today's catalogue.

How the work is done

  1. Wireframes approved by the Operations & Development lead and the Sales Manager (the clickable canvas).
  2. Front end built against test data while the back end connects to the Odoo copy, so neither waits for the other.
  3. Every change reviewed by the other developer, kept in the company's GitHub, with automated tests.
  4. Each phase tested on staging by the Sales Manager against real cases, then signed off before going live.
10 · Build order and the next step

Eight phases, each released only after a sign-off

What the CEO is asked to approve

  • The budget and the two developers (front end and back end), with code owned by Box for Less.
  • Lifting the hold on going online: a host, a company domain and HTTPS.
  • Odoo API access, a staging copy of Odoo and a user seat for the integration user.

What the sales team is asked to do

  • Clean the Odoo data: merge duplicate customers, give every customer a salesperson, a price class, a credit limit (credit customers) and a phone or email.
  • Fill the Brand field on every product, for brand visibility rules and sales by brand.
  • Approve the wireframes and the customer-facing wording: privacy notice, order terms, clearance and shelf-life rules.
  • Enter monthly targets per rep, and test each phase on staging before it goes live.
Detailed rules, Odoo field lists and every acceptance checklist are in the technical specification; the full readiness checklist is its fourth tab.
